The `[MODEL]` is a two-seat, German type-certified ultralight helicopter with a **coaxial main rotor**: two counter-rotating rotor planes on one mast. ### 1.3 Why coaxial (feature blocks)
1. **No tail rotor, fewer hazards.** The two rotor planes counter-rotate, so the torque cancels out and the tail rotor goes away. That removes one of the most common causes of ground and flight accidents in conventional helicopters, and makes ground handling around the aircraft safer.
2. **All the power goes into lift.** Conventional helicopters lose a share of engine power to driving a tail rotor. Here the full engine output turns the main rotor, which gives noticeably more lift for the weight class.
3. **Teetering rotor, longer service life.** Each rotor plane has two blades on a central flapping hinge (a teetering design). This keeps the centre of gravity stable while the blades turn and takes load off the blades and mast, so the structure lasts longer and the aircraft is more reliable.
4. **Better autorotation margin.** With 2 × 2 blades, the rotor system stores much more kinetic energy than a single two-blade rotor. That gives the pilot more time and control in an autorotation.
5. **A precision rotor mast.** The mast carries both rotor planes and is built from a mix of aerospace materials under a protective coating. It is the engineering core of the aircraft.
6. **Composite rotor blades.** The blades were developed for this aircraft and are made from fibre-composite materials, for long service life and consistent flight performance.

### 1.4 Visibility and cabin
A large windscreen, lower front windows and full-height door glazing give an unusually wide field of view. The aircraft can also be flown **with the doors off** for an open side view, which suits aerial photography, survey and observation work as well as summer flying.

### 1.5 Cockpit and avionics
The cockpit comes in several configurations, from a classic round-gauge panel to a full glass display with an EFIS screen. Instrument layout, avionics and comfort options are configured to each customer. (The brochure shows three panel variants on page 9.)

### 3.5 FAQ (draft)
1. **What is a coaxial ultralight helicopter?** A two-seat helicopter in the UL weight class (600 kg MTOW) whose two main rotors turn in opposite directions on a single mast, so no tail rotor is needed.
2. **Why is there no tail rotor?** The counter-rotating rotors cancel each other's torque, so there's nothing for a tail rotor to correct.
3. **How fast and how high can it fly?** Up to 150 km/h cruise, 170 km/h VNE, and a 12,000 ft service ceiling.
4. **What engine does it use, and how much fuel does it burn?** A 160 hp ULPower UL390iS six-cylinder with fuel injection and dual ignition. It burns about 19 l/h of 98-octane gasoline from a 70 l tank.
5. **Is it certified in Germany?** Yes. It holds a German type certificate in the UL-helicopter class, issued in June 2023.
6. **Can it fly without doors?** Yes. It can be operated doors-off for an open side view, for example for aerial photography.
7. **Can it be converted to optionally piloted or autonomous operation?** *(Needs your confirmation before publishing.)*
8. **How do I get a quote or a demo flight?** Use the enquiry form; we reply with the spec sheet and the next steps.
